## Analyst Commentary (Claude Fable 5)
### The Big Picture

Second straight week at the 10-lead target, with cost per lead comfortably under goal and a $26,304 won backyard project tied to a paid click, the largest single ad-attributed win in the dataset. Spend more than doubled and impression share climbed to its highest level in five weeks with no changes made to the account, which points to a summer demand surge that the account rode rather than anything we did. The efficiency cost of that surge is visible: twice the clicks produced the same lead count, so conversion rate roughly halved even though CPA stayed healthy. The structural problem is unchanged: three-quarters or more of eligible auctions are still lost to ad rank in every campaign, so we remain at less than half the impression share target while converting well on the slice we win. One caution: the lead count is slightly inflated again by multiple conversions logged on single clicks, so treat 10 as directionally right, not exact.

### Anomalies & Watch Items

- Spend rose 150% with an empty change log and zero budget-limited impressions. That combination means more people searched and our ads entered more auctions, likely peak irrigation season, not a settings change. Nothing to fix, but expect spend to stay elevated while demand does.
- "haslet landscaping" logged 2 conversions on 1 click, the same double-counting pattern seen July 19. The 50 to 75 percent CTRs on the converting terms are low-volume artifacts, not signals.
- The automated negative list would block "sprinkler repair fort worth" as a search term in the same week the keyword of the same name produced 2 conversions. A phrase negative there could shut off a converting keyword. See Priority Actions.
- "lawn sprinkler repair near me" and "irrigation system repair near me" both fell to quality score 3 in the same week the irrigation campaign absorbed 242 percent more spend. Weakest quality on the campaign carrying the most money is the exact wrong combination.
- The 8p-12a block produced 6 of the 10 conversions, confirming last week's reversal: any schedule that excludes late evening must not exist. Meanwhile 8a-12p spent the second-most with zero conversions, but that block converts in other weeks, so no schedule action is warranted; the pattern flips weekly.
- The promo read turned positive for the first time: irrigation CTR is now above the pre-promo baseline and the conversion rate is well above it, though CTR still trails the control campaigns. One or two more weeks like this and the promo earns its keep.
- Minor data note: this report shows last week's spend as $193 but last week's report said $179. Small enough to ignore, but worth knowing the two sources disagree.
- Last week's three open questions to the web dev team (the negative keyword removed July 14, the three keyword status changes, and the new CommercialMaint schedule hours) show no answer in the log and remain unresolved.
