## Analyst Commentary (Claude Fable 5)
### The Big Picture
This is the best week in the dataset: seven leads at $59 each, the first time cost per lead has beaten the $80 target since the inflated July weeks, and closed ad-sourced work came in at about five times the week's spend. All seven leads came from the irrigation campaign, which converted at $45 per lead, while the form campaign spent $95 and produced nothing. The likely driver is the owner's August 21-22 ad rewrites finally getting a clean week, and the irrigation promo posted its strongest read yet with conversion rate more than four times the pre-promo baseline. The problem has not changed: we still show up in fewer than one in five eligible searches, every lost auction is lost to ad rank and none to budget, so the leads exist but competitors are taking most of them. With spend frozen, quality score and ad copy remain the only levers to widen that funnel.

### Anomalies & Watch Items
- The form campaign (BH_LeadGen_Form_Search) went zero for seven on conversions while the irrigation campaign carried everything. One week of split like this is not proof, but if it repeats, that campaign's keywords and landing page need a hard look.
- The headline "Fort Worth Sprinkler Repair" is live again at 2.9% CTR on 382 impressions, the weakest high-volume headline for the seventh report running despite repeated removal recommendations. It did carry one conversion this week, but it still drags average CTR down.
- The description "Broken sprinkler system? We find the failed zone..." got the most impressions of any description (286) at just 1.4% CTR with zero conversions, while "Same-day sprinkler system repair... since 2008" ran at 8.1% CTR on far fewer impressions. Google is over-serving the weak one.
- The automated negative list again targets core repair terms ("sprinkler repair", "irrigation system repair", "irrigation system repair near me") in the same week that four irrigation repair jobs closed from ads and "lawn sprinkler repair near me" converted as a search term. Do not apply that list blindly.
- Bookkeeping: this report shows last week's impression share as 15%, but last week's report said 14%. Minor, but the sources disagree again.
- Both delete-then-recreate patterns in the change log (see next section) are the one type of web dev change that can cause real damage, and the log does not show the keyword text for either.

Given this team's history of typo'd keywords and inconsistent match types, the full list needs a read-through. Note the quote marks and unspecified match types the UI normalizes are not the concern; typos and wrong terms are.
- Eleven assets were created Aug 25 with no text logged. Same read-through needed before they run another week in live ads.
